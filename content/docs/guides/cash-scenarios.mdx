On **Treasury**, right under **Next 30 days**, a folded section asks **What if clients pay late?** Open it to see what late or missing receivables, and a change in what bills cost, would do to the operating wallet over the next 30, 60 or 90 days: the first day the balance would run short, the bills due while it is, advice in words, what moves it most, and what the figure is built from. Every member of the workspace can use it, viewers included. It works the same on Arc testnet and on Arc mainnet.

Its line under the title says "Nothing here is saved or paid." It is worked out in your browser from what Treasury already shows: changing a setting asks the server nothing, writes nothing to the ledger and reaches no payment and no decision of the agent. **Safe to spend today**, the agent and every check before a payment still count no receivable, whatever you set here.

## What it starts from

The section counts what **Next 30 days** counts, by the same rules, over up to 90 days:

- **Cash** is the operating wallet's USDC and the USYC reserve's, which comes back to the wallet within seconds. It is the only money counted as held. No bank balance is counted.
- **Bills** you owe that are still open, each on its day: a scheduled bill on the day it is scheduled for, any other on its due day, or today when it is overdue. A bill held for a person counts too.
- **Open milestones**, today: a milestone has no payment day, so it is counted as if paid now, unless its USDC is locked in escrow.
- **Recurring payments not billed yet**: the periods of each running recurring payment that have no bill yet, each on its due day (one already due, today). A period's bill is created up to 7 days before its due day, so over 60 or 90 days most periods are not billed yet. Each is marked "recurring, not billed yet". This is the one way the section differs from **Next 30 days**, which counts only bills already created.
- **Receivables**: your open receivables in USDC, already invoiced. Each adds to the balance only on the day it arrives in that case: as things stand, on its due day, or today when it is overdue.
- **Deals not invoiced yet are not counted.** The workspace keeps no record of them, so nothing is made up for them.
- **EURC** bills and receivables are paid from and into the EURC balance. They are listed as left out, never converted and never added to a USDC figure.

- **Look ahead**: 30, 60 or 90 days. The section opens on 60.
- **Clients pay late by (days)**: from 0 to 90; it opens on 14. A receivable is late from its due day, or from today when it is already overdue. One that would arrive after the days you look ahead does not arrive within them.
- **Change in bill costs (%)**: from -50 to 100. 10 is 10% more, -10 is 10% less. It changes bills and recurring payments; milestones keep their amounts, since they are agreed in USDC.
- **The change applies to**: **Every bill**, or **Bills written in another currency**, the bills shadow mode took in your business's own currency and worked out in USDC at a rate. That choice says how many such bills fall in the days you look ahead.
- **Receivables due in the next 60 days** (or 30, or 90) lists each receivable in those days, with its amount and due day. Set each to **Late**, **On time** or **Never arrives**. Each is **Late** until you change it.

These are settings you choose, not predictions: nothing is weighted by how likely it is.

## Read the result

**As things stand** and **In this scenario** sit side by side. Each says "Runs short on" the first day the balance falls below zero, or "Never short in the next 60 days"; the lowest balance and its day; and "Below the cushion from" the first day the balance falls under the cushion the treasury keeps, 15% of what leaves in the 7 days from that day, when that comes before any short day. Under **In this scenario** is what you changed: how many receivables are late, and how many of those land after the days you look ahead; how many never arrive; and how much more or less the bills cost.

Below them, one line gives the cash today, says "No bank balance is counted.", gives the receivables due in the days you look ahead and how much of them arrives within those days in this scenario, and says "Deals not invoiced yet are not counted."

- **What moves it most** names, by the first short day (or by the lowest day when the balance never runs short), the three sources that pull the balance down most: a payee, with its bills, recurring payments and milestones leaving by then together (a milestone counts for its contractor); a client whose receivable this scenario makes late past that day or drops; and a rise in bill costs, as one source.

A short day is not an instruction to do anything with your money. The section offers no button and moves nothing: what to pay, chase or move stays your decision, made where you make it today.

## What this is built from

**What this is built from** says how much of the picture is known. Its badge, "Bills known for 28 of 60 days" in the picture, counts from today to the latest day a bill is due. Bills nobody has added yet are not counted, so the days after the latest bill may look better than they will be, and the section says so under the list.

The list gives:

- the bills: how many, their total and the latest due day;
- the recurring payments not billed yet: how many, their total, and from how many schedules. When they could not be read, it says "Recurring payments could not be read, so none is counted.";
- the open milestones, counted today;
- the receivables: how many, their total, and how many come from clients who have paid before;
- for each such client, its record: how many receivables it paid on time and how many late, and how many days late on average. A payment is on time when it arrived on or before its due day (UTC), as the agent counts it before it reminds a client to pay. "Client records are counts, not a prediction.";
- what is left out: EURC bills and receivables.

The section ends with "A what-if from the bills and receivables on record, not a forecast."

## When something changes

Treasury reads its rows again every 20 seconds, and the section works out its figures again from them each time, keeping what you set: a bill whose due day moves moves on the chart, a new bill is counted, and a receivable that is paid or removed leaves the list. Closing the page forgets the scenario; nothing is kept.

Not in this version: deals not invoiced yet, a lateness of its own for each client, and saving or sharing a scenario. Telegram's `/today` and Slack's `/vestiarion today` still answer for the next 30 days.
