An outside accountant who looks after several small businesses should not have to open each workspace to find out where something needs attention. Each business invites its accountant as a member with the **accountant** role, or opens its workspace from the accountant's own client link: see [Invite a client](#invite-a-client). The accountant's **Accountant console** then lists every workspace they are a member of, how long its items have been open, and one queue across them: payments waiting for a decision, bills waiting for your co-sign and addresses to confirm where an owner lets accountants co-sign, payments to book in Xero, receivables to follow up, what customers said on their statement links, connectors to fix and limits reached. Each item opens the right page in its own workspace. It works the same in a workspace on Arc testnet and on Arc mainnet.

## 1. Invite your accountant

An owner or admin opens **Members** and, under the invitation form, chooses the role **accountant**. While you run the workspace alone, **Treasury** offers the same invitation under **Bring in a second person**: see [Bring in a second person](/docs/guides/workspace#bring-in-a-second-person). The form says what each role may do before you send it. For the accountant:

> Reads everything, books payments in Xero, applies money received, checks vendors' statements and answers customers. Decides no payment, and changes no treasury, limit, integration or member.

The accountant accepts the invitation with the address it was sent to, as any member does, and lands on the **Accountant console**, at their first step.

What each role may do:

| Role | What it may do |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Everything an admin does, and connects Circle, sets the two-approval figure and can delete the workspace. |
| Admin | Everything an approver and an accountant do, and adds bills and receivables, runs and resumes the agent, and manages members, API keys, webhooks, integrations and treasury. |
| Approver | Decides the payments the agent held, and pauses the agent. Adds no records. |
| Accountant | Reads everything, books payments in Xero, applies money received, checks vendors' statements and answers customers. Decides no payment, and changes no treasury, limit, integration or member. |
| Viewer | Reads the workspace. |

- **Owners and admins invite an accountant,** as they invite an approver or a viewer. An accountant invites nobody and changes no role.
- **To give your accountant more,** change their role on **Members**: as an approver they decide payments, as an admin they also manage the workspace. One person holds one role in a workspace.
- **Deciding payments stays with your people.** An accountant's card in **Approvals** shows who can decide each bill; the accountant can read it all and pay, reject or return none of it. An owner can let accountants co-sign larger bills as the second person: see [Co-sign a larger bill](#co-sign-a-larger-bill).

### Your first steps

The **Accountant console** shows a new accountant **Get started as an accountant**, four steps ticked because each happened, in any of the workspaces they keep the books of:

1. **Open the accountant console**: done when the console opens.
2. **Open one workspace's queue**: **Only this one**, on a workspace's card.
3. **Book one payment or apply one receipt**: it opens the bookings to review where a payment waits for you, and otherwise the **Receivables** tab, to apply money a customer sent.
4. **Download the month's hand-off**: last month's CSV; the download is recorded in the workspace's ledger, which is what ticks it.

An optional fifth step, **Invite a client**, is ticked once one of your client links is used. The checklist is done without it.

**Don't show again** hides it for good. No step needs more than the accountant role may do.

### Invite a client

An accountant can also bring a new client in. On the **Accountant console**, under **Invite a client**, choose **Create link**. Give the client's email first and the link works only for that address. The link is shown once, with **Copy link**: send it to your client yourself, by email or chat.

- **Who can.** Someone who is an accountant in at least one workspace. Until then, ask a business to invite you on its **Members**. If you are no longer an accountant in any workspace, you cannot make a new link, but the console still lists your links while one is open, so you can revoke it. It says "You are no longer an accountant in any workspace, so you cannot make a new link. A link you made still works until it is used, expires or you revoke it."
- **One link, one client.** A link works once, for 7 days. You can hold 10 open links at a time and make 20 a day, revoked ones counted. Only the link's SHA-256 is kept, so a lost link cannot be shown again: make a new one.
- **What your client sees.** They sign in first, with the address the link is bound to when it is one. The page names you by the address you sign in with: "*your email* invites you to keep your books in Vestiarion. They will be your workspace's accountant:", then what an accountant may do, a **Workspace name**, and a box to tick: "Let *your email* into this workspace as its accountant. I can remove them from Members at any time." **Open my workspace** opens it.
- **Their workspace.** It is theirs: they are its owner. It opens on Arc testnet in shadow mode, for bills in USDC, and you are in it as its accountant from the start. It shows on your console at once, with your checklist. Its ledger signs `org_created` with `via: "accountant_link"`, then your `member_joined` with the link's id. Nothing is written in your other workspaces.
- **Your links.** The console lists your links, newest first: **Open**, with the day it expires and **Revoke**; **Used**, naming the workspace while you are still a member of it; **Expired**; **Revoked**.
- **A link that offers nothing.** A link bound to another address says "This link was made for a different email address. Sign in with that address to use it." A used, expired or revoked link shows one sentence and names no one. Opened by you, your own link says "This is your own client link. Send it to your client."
- **If you were not added.** A link that stopped working before your client chose **Open my workspace** opens nothing and says why. Should their workspace be made but you not be added, because the link was used or revoked at that very moment, or the request failed, they keep the workspace and their console says "Your accountant was not added." with **Invite them on Members**. A link that was still open stays open.
- **When they go live.** Their live slice on Arc mainnet offers to bring you along: see [Start the live slice](/docs/guides/report#start-the-live-slice).

Until the deployment has client links set up, the console says "Client invite links are not available yet."

## 2. What an accountant does in a workspace

- **Book payments made on Arc in Xero,** after reviewing each, from **Bills & receivables**. On a Xero connection made before Oct 13, 2026, only an owner or admin books until one of them reconnects Xero, and the console says so on each payment to book. See [Bring bills in from Xero](/docs/guides/xero).
- **Apply money received to receivables,** record a fee, and reverse a mistake. See [Apply money received to invoices](/docs/guides/apply-payments). Anyone else reads "Only an owner, admin or accountant of this workspace can apply payments."
- **Answer what customers said** on their statement links, under **From customers** on the **Receivables** tab: reply, mark a payment found, or close it as not received. See [Share a statement with a customer](/docs/guides/customer-portal). Making and stopping a statement link stays with owners and admins.
- **Get an email when a customer writes,** with the switch in **Settings**, under **Notifications**: "Email me when a customer writes on its statement link".
- **Check a vendor's statement** against the bills and the payments made to them: see [Check a vendor's statement](#check-a-vendor-s-statement).
- **Co-sign a larger bill** and confirm a changed address, where an owner lets accountants co-sign: see [Co-sign a larger bill](#co-sign-a-larger-bill).
- **Read everything else,** the ledger, the report, Treasury and the audit export included.

### Check a vendor's statement

At month end a vendor sends a statement of account: the invoices they billed, the payments they received, and what they say is owed. On **Counterparties**, a vendor's row has **Check their statement**, for owners, admins and accountants:

1. **Give it the statement.** Choose its CSV, or paste its table from their portal, a spreadsheet or a PDF. It is read as a bank statement is: the delimiter, the header row and the columns are found, and when a date or an amount reads two ways, you are asked. The columns are the date, the invoice number, a description if it has one, the charges and the payments, or one amount column, where a charge is a positive amount unless you say otherwise, and the balance, where DR is what you owe. Type its currency when no column gives it.
2. **Check against your books.** Each charge is matched to the vendor's bill with the same invoice number, ignoring spaces, dashes and case, or whose memo starts with it. Only a charge with no number, or a bill with none, is matched by amount instead: the bill of the same amount added closest in time, within 45 days. Each payment they list is matched to one of yours of the same amount within 10 days: what you recorded you paid, where you did, as in shadow mode, or else a payment made here. USD is compared with USDC, EUR with EURC, and a bill written in your own currency in shadow mode by that figure.
3. **Read what is left.**
   - **Missing here**: their charges with no bill. An owner or admin can add each from the line, with its number, amount and description filled in; it goes through every check, as a bill typed in.
   - **Rejected here**: their charges for a bill you rejected, to tell them why or ask them to take it off.
   - **Listed twice**: the same number twice on their statement, or on two of your bills.
   - **Amount differs**: theirs, yours and the difference.
   - **Paid, not on their statement**: what you paid them in those days that they have not listed. A payment on Arc mainnet comes with its transaction's link, to send them.
   - **A payment you have no record of**: one they list that matches none of yours.
   - **Not on their statement**: your bills added in those days that they do not list.
   - Their last printed balance beside what your open bills to them add up to by the statement's last day. A charge matched to a bill in another currency is named, since its amount was not compared.

Nothing is paid or changed, and the file is not kept. The check is signed into the ledger as `vendor_statement_checked`, with its counts and balances only.

### Co-sign a larger bill

Above the workspace's figure for two approvals, a bill needs two people. A founder who runs the workspace alone has only one, so an owner can make the accountant the second: in **Settings**, under **Two approvals**, choose **Let accountants co-sign**. Each change is signed as `accountant_cosign_changed`. With it on:

- **Co-sign.** A bill above the figure shows the accountant **Co-sign** in **Approvals**. It asks first, then records one of the bill's two approvals for its amount, currency and address as they stand, and says "Co-signed. An owner, admin or approver's Approve and pay pays it." It is signed as `approval_given`, with `cosign: true`.
- **It never pays.** A co-sign sends nothing, even when another approval is already given. The approval that pays is always an owner's, admin's or approver's **Approve and pay**, and the `approval_paid` entry names both. Below the figure one approval pays, so there is nothing to co-sign.
- **The accountant counts as a second person** for bills. A founder alone with their accountant can turn two approvals on, and pay a bill they entered once the accountant has co-signed it.
- **Confirm address.** The accountant also confirms a changed address on **Counterparties**, with the same record of how it was checked, as the second person the [payee lock](/docs/guides/first-payment#payee-lock) asks for. The agent then pays that address as usual, within its limits.
- **Your console lists them.** The **Accountant console** shows each bill waiting for your co-sign as **Waiting for your co-sign**, and each changed address no one has confirmed as **Address to confirm**, across every workspace that lets you co-sign; **Open** goes to the bill in **Approvals** or the payee on **Counterparties**. A bill you already co-signed, or one that cannot be co-signed yet, stays a payment waiting for a decision and says why. See [What the queue holds](#4-what-the-queue-holds).
- **Only when it counts.** **Co-sign** shows only on a bill your co-sign can still help pay. Where two approvals already stand, or another accountant co-signed it, there is nothing to add. For the usual reasons a co-sign would be refused, the card says why in its place: "Waiting for a verdict in shadow mode", "Someone disagreed with it in shadow mode", "Being decided right now", "Screened high risk", "The payee has no payment address yet" (where payments are real), "The payee is frozen", "Confirm the address first" or "Waiting for the payee lock's wait to end". A few are refused only when you click **Co-sign**, which then says why. The main one is a first payment to an address you confirmed that no member answers for alone, such as one sent on GitHub: someone else must approve it.
- **What it does not cover.** Contractors' milestones are not co-signed: above the figure, a milestone still needs two people who can approve payments. Turning it off, or changing the accountant's role, makes their co-signs count for nothing.

## 3. Open the console

Open **Accountant console** from the workspaces page, which lists it when you are in more than one workspace or an accountant in one, or go to `/accountant`. It lists only the workspaces you are a member of, each read with your role there. It shows nothing from a workspace you were not invited to, and no figure that adds money across workspaces or currencies: it counts items, and each amount stays on its own item, in its own currency.

- **Open items by age** counts what is open across your workspaces in five buckets: **Under a day**, **1 to 3 days**, **4 to 7 days**, **8 to 30 days** and **Over 30 days**. An item's age runs from when it needed someone: the agent's decision for a held payment, its decide-by day once that has passed (a bill waiting for your co-sign too), a receivable's due date, a customer's message, a payment's booking draft, the change of an address to confirm.
- **Workspaces** has a card for each: its standing, network and your role, how many items are open and of which kind, **Open workspace**, and **Only this one** to narrow the queue to it. A part that could not be read just now is named on the card, and the rest still shows.
- **What needs attention** is the queue. What you can act on comes first, then by kind, then the oldest first. **Yours to act on** keeps only those; each kind has its own filter.

## 4. What the queue holds

Each item names its workspace, who or what it is about, its amount in its own currency where it has one, how long it has been open, and who acts on it: "You can ..." when your role may, or which roles may when it does not. **Open** goes to the page where it is acted on.

| Kind | What it is | Opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Past its decide-by day | A payment held for a person whose day to decide passed: its due date, or the last day of its early-payment discount. A late fee never moves the day: the item names the fee, "Its decide-by day was Oct 20, 2026; a late fee of 1.5% a month starts Oct 30, 2026", or "applies since" once it has. | **Approvals**, at the bill |
| Payment waiting for a decision | A payable held, flagged or waiting for details, or a milestone held for a person. For a co-signing accountant, a bill already co-signed, or one that cannot be co-signed yet, which says why. | **Approvals**, or **Contractors** for a milestone |
| Waiting for your co-sign | A bill above the figure for two approvals that you can co-sign now, where an owner lets accountants co-sign. It says the figure and whether one approval is given. | **Approvals**, at the bill |
| Address to confirm | A payee whose address changed and no one has confirmed it, for whoever may confirm it: owners, admins and approvers, and accountants where an owner lets them co-sign. A frozen payee waits on an owner or admin, who unfreezes it first. Under the payee lock, an address you entered or asked for waits on another member, unless no one else can confirm it. The address itself is not shown. | **Counterparties**, at the payee |
| Customer says paid | A customer said on its statement link that it paid. Nothing is marked paid until the money is found. | **From customers** |
| Disputed by the customer | A customer disputes an invoice. Its reminders wait while the dispute is open. | **From customers** |

| Receivable overdue | A receivable past its due date and not yet received. | The receivable |

With nothing open, the queue says "Nothing needs attention in any of your workspaces."

## 5. Hand a month over

Each workspace card has **Hand-off:** downloads: last month as CSV or JSON, and **This month so far** as CSV. A hand-off holds, for that calendar month (UTC):

- **Decisions**: the agent's decisions on payables and a person's approvals, rejections and returns, an accountant's co-signs among them, marked `co-sign` in the CSV, each with its ledger number and hash, from the same signed ledger the [audit export](/docs/guides/audit-export) holds. The hand-off names the ledger's last entry, so the audit export of the same workspace verifies it.
- **Payments** sent or confirmed in the month: what they paid, the amount and token, the payee's address, whether they moved on chain, and the transaction.
- **Bookings** of payments made in the month, in Xero: their state, reference, account and amount in their own currency.
- **Open items**: the console's own items for the workspace when you download it, each with its link.

The CSV is one table with a `section` column, in UTF-8 for a spreadsheet; the JSON is one document, `vestiarion-handoff/1`. Amounts keep their own currency in every row, and nothing totals across currencies. Every member may download a workspace's hand-off, as every member reads the workspace. Each download is recorded in the workspace's ledger as `handoff_exported`, with who downloaded it, the month and how many rows of each section it held. Past ten in ten minutes, the download answers "Too many hand-offs in the last 10 minutes. Try again later."

### The monthly close pack

The **Monthly close pack** is a workspace's month in one table: every payment Circle confirmed in it, one row each, with the bill it paid, who decided and approved it, what was checked before it was sent, the transaction, and the signed ledger entry that records it, checked against the workspace's keys. It reads the ledger and the payments: it decides nothing, pays nothing, and no model reads it.

Every member can open it, viewers included, as every member reads the evidence pages. In a workspace, open **Audit log** and choose **Monthly close pack**, beside the audit export. On the **Accountant console**, each workspace card has a **Close pack:** line under its hand-off, with last month and **This month so far**.

- **The month.** A calendar month in UTC, from the 1st at 00:00 UTC up to the next month's 1st, as the hand-off and **Usage this month** count it. A payment counts in the month Circle confirmed it. The page opens on last month: choose another with the **Month** field and **Show this month**, or with **Last month**, **This month so far** and the months either side. This month reads "so far" until it ends. A month that cannot be read shows last month and says "That month could not be read, so this shows last month. Choose a month with the Month field."
- **The figures.** **Payments settled**, **Paid** for each token apart (USDC and EURC are never added together), **Route fees** (what a CCTP or Gateway payout cost, in USDC), **Not settled** and **Signed entries verified**. A sandbox's simulated payments are listed and totalled apart, as they move nothing on chain. A payment made in shadow mode is a real transfer that mirrors a bill your business paid its own way: it counts with the rest, and the page says how many there were. Network fees are listed on each payment, with where the figure came from, and never added up, since a batch's fee belongs to the whole batch.
- **Each payment.** Its bill, with the vendor's invoice number, the amount, the due date, an early-payment discount, the purchase order and the SHA-256 of the document it was read from (the document itself is not kept). The address paid, in full, on its chain, or a bank payout by its bank code and the last 4 digits of the account. What the agent decided, and who approved it, the earlier approval first: a bill the agent paid within its rules reads "No person approved it: code checked it and the agent paid it."; an accountant's co-sign reads "Co-signed by", then the approver who paid it. Who gave the address and who confirmed it follow, and "A second person stood behind it" says whether someone other than whoever gave it confirmed it.
- **Who is named.** People are named by email, with the role they hold now: the role someone held when they approved is not signed in the ledger. Someone who has left is a former member, with the first 8 characters of their id.
- **The checks.** Six on each payment, labelled the way the evidence page labels a fact, from **Checked by code** and **Verified outside** to **Missing** and **Does not match**: **Guardrails**, what code checked before it was sent; **Screening**, the payee's newest screening before the decision; **OFAC address list**, whether the address paid was on OFAC's SDN list; **Payee lock**, the address's confirmation and its wait, under the lock in force when it was sent, so a payment an owner let go by easing the lock reads who eased it and when; **Invoice's own address**, whether the address on the bill's document is the one paid; and **Milestone**, how a contractor's work was verified. A check with nothing to look at for that payment, such as the payee lock for a bank payout, reads "Does not apply".
- **The transaction and its record.** How it was paid (a transfer, a CCTP mint with its burn, a Gateway payout, an escrow release, a bank payout or a simulated payment), its hash written out in full with **View on the explorer**, its fees and any earlier attempts. Then whether the bill is marked paid with it, and its booking in Xero; the signed entry that records it, with its number, its key id and whether it verified; and every entry the row rests on. **Open the payment's evidence** opens its evidence page, which also reads the transaction on chain.
- **Not settled in the month.** Under "Not settled in" and the month: each payment created or sent in the month that Circle did not confirm in it, with its state. It settled in a later month, and counts in that month's pack; it waits to be sent; it was sent and is not confirmed yet; its outcome is unknown; it failed, was cancelled or was not sent; or BlindPay refunded a bank payout. None is in the totals. Money locked in escrow for a milestone, or taken back, is listed under **Escrow**, apart from the totals too: a lock pays no one, and a release that paid the contractor is a payment.
- **What it leaves out.** It lists payments Vestiarion made. A bill marked paid with no payment, such as sample data or history imported as paid, is on its own evidence page, which says "Marked paid, with no payment recorded."; money received is under **Receivables** (see [Apply money received to invoices](/docs/guides/apply-payments)); a treasury move into or out of [USYC](/docs/guides/go-live#earn-on-idle-cash-with-usyc) is in the **Audit log**; what a business in shadow mode paid its own way is in the report's [Agent vs what really happened](/docs/guides/report#agent-vs-what-really-happened); and a booking made in the accounting tool without a payment is in the tool itself. A payment whose bill was deleted is counted, not listed, and the audit export holds its entries. A month holds at most 2,000 payments, settled and not settled together: past that, the pack shows the first 2,000 and says so.

Three ways take the month out:

- **Print or save as PDF** opens your browser's print dialog; choose Save as PDF there. The printed page leaves out the workspace's menus, the month controls, the buttons and the links. It adds a line at the top, "Vestiarion monthly close pack", with the workspace, its network, the month and when it was read, and keeps each payment on one page where it fits.
- **Download CSV** gives one row per payment settled in the month, for a spreadsheet: the payment (`paid_at`, `payment_id`, `record`, `record_id`), the payee (`payee`, `payee_id`, `paid_to`, `chain`), the bill (`invoice_number`, `milestone`, `bill_amount`, `bill_currency`, `amount_paid`, `token`, `discount`, `due_date`, `po_reference`, `document_sha256`, `vendor_document_sha256`, `simulated`, `shadow`), the approvals (`decided`, `approvals`, `authorized_by`, `address_given_by`, `address_confirmed_by`, `second_person`), the checks (`guardrails`, `guardrail_notes`, `screening`, `wallet_list`, `payee_lock`, `pay_to`, `milestone_verification`), the transaction (`route`, `tx_hash`, `tx_url`, `burn_tx_hash`, `network_fee_usd`, `network_fee_source`, `route_fee_usdc`, `earlier_attempts`), the books (`books`, `booked_in`), the signed entry (`entry_seq`, `entry_hash`, `signature`, `signing_key_id`, `entry_verified`), and `evidence_url` and `entries`. A check reads its label, a colon and its sentence; yes or no columns read `yes`, `no`, or nothing when it is not known; `entry_verified` reads `yes`, `no` or `not checked`. A cell that starts like a spreadsheet formula is prefixed with `'`, so it never runs, and the file starts with a byte-order mark, so Excel reads it as UTF-8. Payments not settled and escrow moves are on the page and in the JSON.
- **Download signed JSON** gives the file that can be checked, in the format `vestiarion-close-pack/1`.

Files are named for the workspace and the month, `vestiarion-close-pack-your-workspace-2026-10.csv` or `.json`, with `-so-far` before the extension for a month not ended, so an early copy is never taken for the closed month.

Each download is recorded in the workspace's ledger as `close_pack_exported`, with who downloaded it (by id, never by email), the month, whether it had ended, the format, how many payments it held settled and not settled, the SHA-256 of the file, and the ledger's last entry when it was read. The file's own SHA-256 equals that entry's `sha256`, so a copy can be matched to its download later; on Windows, `Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256` prints it in capitals. Opening the page and printing it record nothing. Past ten downloads by one person in ten minutes, the download answers "Too many close pack downloads in the last 10 minutes. Try again later." Past six downloads of one workspace's pack within a minute, in any tab, the next waits: it answers "Too many close pack downloads in a row. Try again in a minute." One more is served each minute after that. Someone signed out is told "Sign in to download this close pack." The page itself reads ten times at once, then once every 6 seconds, since each read checks every entry's signature; past that it says "The close pack was read many times in a row. Try again in a minute." The files name members by email and payees by name, as the page does: share them with whoever should see that.

To check the signed JSON without trusting Vestiarion:

1. **Read what it holds.** `format` and `exportedAt`, when the pack was read; `workspace`, `network` and `month`, with `ended`; `ledgerHead`, the ledger's last entry then; `totals`; `rows`, one per settled payment with its `bill`, `approval`, `checks`, `transaction`, `record` and `books`, each fact naming its entries by `seq`; `notSettled` and `escrow`; `labels`, what each check's label means; `checkAnEntry`; `keys`, the workspace's public keys; and `entries`, every entry the rows cite, once each, oldest first.
2. **Compare the keys** with the ones the **Audit log** shows beside **Ledger signing public key**, as [Verify an audit export](/docs/guides/audit-export#4-compare-the-key-id-and-the-head) explains.
3. **Check each entry alone,** as for the [payee lock's evidence pack](/docs/guides/evidence-and-receipts#6-check-the-signed-json-offline): its `body_hash`, its `signature` by the key `signing_key_id` names, and its `hash`. A developer can run each entry through the [SDK's](/docs/get-started/sdk#webhooks) `verifyLedgerEntry`, with `body_hash`, `prev_hash` and `signing_key_id` named `bodyHash`, `prevHash` and `signingKeyId`, and the keys as a map of id to PEM.
4. **Place the entries in the whole ledger.** Download the [audit export](/docs/guides/audit-export) and check it; each entry in the pack must have the same `seq` and `hash` there.
5. **Tie the file to its download.** The file itself is not signed: the ledger key signs ledger entries only. Its download's `close_pack_exported` entry is signed, and holds the file's SHA-256.

### Export the close pack to Google Sheets

A month's close pack can go straight into a spreadsheet in a Google Drive, instead of a CSV you import by hand. Vestiarion makes one spreadsheet for each workspace and month, writes the pack into it, and records each export in the workspace's signed ledger. It writes a copy: it reads nothing back into Vestiarion, decides nothing, and no model sees it.

**Connect a Google account.** An owner or admin opens **Settings**, finds **Google Sheets** among the integrations and chooses **Connect Google Sheets**. The connect page says what Vestiarion does with the Google Drive; tick **I agree** and choose **Continue to Google**. Google's screen then names Vestiarion and asks for one permission, in Google's words: "See, edit, create, and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app". This is Google's `drive.file` permission: Vestiarion reaches only the spreadsheets it makes, and cannot see, open or list any other file in that Drive. The spreadsheets go to the My Drive of the Google account you choose there, and the card reads **Connected to the Google Drive of** that account, its email masked. Leave the Google Drive box ticked on Google's screen: without it, nothing is connected.

A workspace connects one Google account. The same account can be connected to several workspaces, and each keeps its own spreadsheets.

**Export a month.** An owner, admin or accountant opens **Monthly close pack**, picks the month and chooses **Export to Google Sheets**, under the downloads. The answer counts the payments written, for example "Exported 14 payments to Google Sheets.", names the Drive, and links **Open the spreadsheet**, which opens it in a new tab. The page then shows when the month was last exported, with the same link, to every member. Approvers and viewers see that line but no button; they can still download the CSV.

The spreadsheet is named for the workspace, the month and the network, such as "Vestiarion close pack · Pinecrest Studio · October 2026 so far · Arc testnet". It has two tabs:

- **Payments** holds the same 51 columns as the CSV, one row per payment settled in the month, under a frozen, bold header. The amounts (`bill_amount`, `amount_paid`, `discount`, `network_fee_usd` and `route_fee_usdc`) and `earlier_attempts` are numbers, so a column adds up; every other cell is text. Each cell is written as a value, never as a formula: a payee or an invoice number that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` stays that text, with nothing added in front.
- **Summary** names the workspace, the network, the month, when the pack was read and who exported it; the payments settled and what they paid in each token; how many payments were not settled and how many escrow moves there were, which the close pack page lists; the signed entries verified, the ledger's last entry and the signing keys; the **Payments tab SHA-256**; a link to the close pack page; and how to check it all.

Exporting the same month again updates that spreadsheet: **Payments** and **Summary** are written afresh, and either one renamed or deleted is added back. A tab you added yourself is left alone, but cells you changed in those two are overwritten. This month's spreadsheet says "so far" in its name, and the export after the month ends renames it. Renaming or moving the spreadsheet in Drive keeps it in step; a copy of it is never updated, so export again rather than edit a copy. A spreadsheet in the trash, or deleted, is replaced by a new one at the next export.

**Check it.** Each row of **Payments** names the signed ledger entry that records its payment, in `entry_seq`, `entry_hash`, `signature` and `signing_key_id`: download the month's signed JSON and check those entries as above. Each export is recorded in the ledger as `close_pack_exported`, with `format` `google_sheets`, the spreadsheet's id (the long id in its address) and a `sha256` equal to the Summary's **Payments tab SHA-256**. That is the SHA-256 of the **Payments** tab as written, so anyone with the spreadsheet can compute it: open **Extensions**, then **Apps Script**, paste this function and run it.

```js
function paymentsSha256() {
  const rows = SpreadsheetApp.getActive().getSheetByName("Payments").getDataRange().getValues();
  const digest = Utilities.computeDigest(Utilities.DigestAlgorithm.SHA_256, JSON.stringify(rows), Utilities.Charset.UTF_8);
  return digest.map((b) => ((b + 256) % 256).toString(16).padStart(2, "0")).join("");
}
```

It returns the same hex as long as no one has changed the **Payments** tab since the export. Before recording an export as done, Vestiarion reads the tab back once and compares it. When it does not match, the export answers "The spreadsheet did not read back as written, so the export is not recorded as done. Export again." An export that failed once rows may have reached Google is recorded too, as `close_pack_export_failed`, with the stage it stopped at and why.

The spreadsheet names members by email and payees by name, as the close pack does: share it with whoever should see that. Vestiarion never shares it: the Google account that holds it decides who opens it.

**Limits and messages.** Exports and downloads share one limit: past ten by one person in ten minutes, an export answers "Too many close pack exports in the last 10 minutes. Try again later." Several exports in a row from different tabs answer "Too many close pack exports in a row. Try again in a minute." When Google asks for a pause, the export says "Google asked Vestiarion to slow down. Try again in a minute." A full Drive, or Drive turned off by a Google Workspace administrator, is said in words too.

When Google stops accepting the connection, because its access was removed in the Google Account or it went unused for six months, the card says **Google Sheets needs you to reconnect** and an owner or admin chooses **Reconnect Google Sheets**. The accountant console lists it as a **Connector problem** until then.

**Disconnect.** An owner or admin chooses **Disconnect Google Sheets** on the card. Vestiarion revokes its access at Google and deletes the tokens. The spreadsheets already exported stay in that Drive: Vestiarion never deletes one. When another workspace is connected with the same Google account, Vestiarion does not revoke, since that would cut the other workspace off too, and says so; once none is left, remove Vestiarion from **Third-party apps & services** in that Google Account. Deleting a workspace deletes its tokens without telling Google, so remove Vestiarion there as well.

**Before the update.** Until the deployment's operator runs migration 0145, the card says "Google Sheets is not set up on this deployment yet." and the close pack page shows no export button. A deployment with no Google client set up shows no Google Sheets card at all.

## 6. Usage and limits

Every member sees **Usage this month** in **Settings**: what the workspace used since the 1st (UTC), as **Model calls**, **Circle transfers**, **x402 purchases**, **Emails** and **Connector syncs**, each split by what it was for. A model call shows the tokens the provider reported and its cost in US dollars where the model's price is known; an x402 purchase shows its price in USDC; a Circle transfer shows its network fee where the chain reported it. Costs are never added across currencies, and "This is a record, not a bill."

Under **Limits**, each limit that applies says where the workspace stands (**Within limit**, **Near the limit** or **Stopped**), what stops when it is reached, and **Who acts:**

- **Agent cycles today**, in a sandbox: at most 20 cycles a day (UTC). Past it, cycles wait for midnight UTC.
- **Agent spending limit**: past it, the agent pays nothing on its own, and each payment waits in **Approvals**. An owner or admin raises the limit on the console.
- **Payee history checks (x402)**: the agent's daily most, and the Gateway balance it buys from. Past either, it decides without buying.
- **Invitations**: at most 50 a day. Past it, no new invitation is sent until the oldest of the day is 24 hours old.
- A connector that needs reconnecting is listed too, with who reconnects it.

A limit that stopped something also shows at the top of the section, and in the accountant console's queue as **Limit reached**, so it never goes unseen.

## 7. When an accountant leaves

Remove the accountant on **Members**, or they leave on their own. The next time they open the console, the workspace is no longer listed, its hand-off download answers as for any workspace they never belonged to, and its pages say it was not found. Nothing was copied to them ahead: the console and the hand-off are read for each request, from the workspace, with the role they hold then. The API keys they created in the workspace, if an owner had made them an admin, are revoked with the membership, as for any member.
