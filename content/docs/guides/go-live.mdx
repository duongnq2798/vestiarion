A workspace starts in sandbox. Going live gives it treasury wallets on its network and sets the agent to pay from them on its own schedule. On Arc testnet they are funded with testnet USDC from Circle's faucet. On Arc mainnet they hold real USDC, or the treasury can be a wallet you hold yourself, and [On Arc mainnet](#on-arc-mainnet) says what differs there. This page takes you through it, from **Settings** to the confirmation.

Only an **owner** of the workspace can take it live. Everyone else sees the workspace's status in the same place, with the line "An owner can connect Circle and take this workspace live from here."

## Before you start

Until the workspace has made its first payment on Arc testnet, the console shows owners and admins a **Get started** checklist: add a wallet, fund it with USDC, go live, add a payee with an Arc address, add a payable, and the first payment. Each step ticks itself off from the workspace's own records, and the next one links to the page where it is done. **Read the guide** opens this page until the workspace is live and funded, and [Your first payment](/docs/guides/first-payment) after that. The checklist disappears once the workspace is live and its first payment has settled.

Open your workspace and go to **Settings**, at `/o/<slug>/settings`. **Go live** opens the **Workspace** group, and its badge shows where the workspace is. The section shows one step of three at a time:

1. Choose where the wallets live.
2. Create the treasury wallets.
3. Fund the operating wallet, then go live.

On Arc mainnet, step 1 can make a wallet you hold the treasury instead. [Path C](#path-c-your-own-wallet) has its own steps 2 and 3.

## 1. Choose where the wallets live

Step 1 depends on the deployment:

- **When the deployment has hosted wallets**, step 1 is "Choose where the wallets live". It offers a hosted testnet wallet first (path A), and your own Circle account under "Connect your own Circle account" (path B).
- **On Arc mainnet, when the deployment offers your own wallet**, step 1 is "Choose where the treasury lives". It offers your own wallet first (path C), and your own Circle account under "Connect your own Circle account" (path B).
- **Otherwise**, step 1 is "Connect your Circle account" and shows only the form for your own account. Follow path B.

### Path A: a Vestiarion testnet wallet

You don't need a Circle account for this path. The workspace's wallets are created in Vestiarion's own Circle testnet account, in a wallet set of their own.

1. Under "A testnet wallet, no Circle account needed", choose **Use a Vestiarion testnet wallet**. The line under the button reads "Hosted by Vestiarion · Arc testnet".
2. The page confirms: "This workspace will use a Vestiarion testnet wallet; create its treasury wallets next."

Until the wallets are created, you can still change your mind. Open **Connect your own Circle account instead** and follow path B. Once the wallets exist, the choice is fixed, and the page says "To use your own Circle account, start a new workspace."

### Path B: your own Circle account

For this path you need two values from your account in the [Circle developer console](https://console.circle.com):

- **An API key.** Create a testnet API key in the console, because the workspace's wallets are on Arc testnet.
- **An entity secret.** The entity secret is the key that secures developer-controlled wallets. Generate it and **register it in the Circle console first**, as [Circle's guide to registering an entity secret](https://developers.circle.com/wallets/dev-controlled/register-entity-secret) describes, and keep its recovery file safe. Vestiarion does not register it for you. The field asks for "The one registered for developer-controlled wallets."

Then, in **Go live**:

1. Open **Connect your own Circle account**, if the step shows the hosted choice first.
2. Paste the key into **API key** and the secret into **Entity secret**.
3. Choose **Connect Circle**. Circle checks the API key before anything is stored. When it accepts the key, the page says "Circle is connected." Both values are then encrypted, and they are never shown again.

The entity secret itself is checked in step 2, when the wallets are created. To change either value later, open **Replace Circle credentials**. The new values must belong to the same Circle account, because the workspace's wallets are in it.

Connecting also adds a webhook to your Circle account, at `https://www.vestiarion.xyz/api/circle/notifications`, for transfers out and in. You can see it under **Webhooks** in Circle's console. Circle uses it to tell Vestiarion when a transfer settles, so a payment it confirms after the agent stopped waiting is recorded within seconds instead of at the next cycle, while the agent runs. Vestiarion reads the transfer back from Circle before it records anything. Removing the webhook only slows that down: the agent's cycles still settle every payment.

### Path C: your own wallet

On Arc mainnet, where the deployment offers it, step 1 can put the treasury in a wallet you hold, and you need no Circle account. The workspace's agent can then pay from your wallet only through a contract your wallet deploys and approves, within the daily and 7-day figures you set in it. Vestiarion never holds your USDC. Path C has its own steps 2 and 3, described here. Sections 2 to 4 below are for paths A and B.

Step 1 offers two kinds of wallet, and leads with the one your browser suits:

- **A wallet in your browser**, such as MetaMask or Rabby. When the browser has one, **Your own wallet** comes first, and its button names the wallet when it says its name, as in "Connect MetaMask".
- **A passkey wallet**, made in Go live with your face, fingerprint or device PIN: Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, or your phone. When the browser has no wallet, **Create a wallet with a passkey** comes first, and a line says how to use MetaMask or Rabby instead. The passkey wallet is a smart account on Arc mainnet owned by your passkey, and Vestiarion holds no key to it.

#### With a wallet in your browser

You need a wallet in your browser holding USDC on Arc mainnet: 0.50 USDC for the agent's gas, a little for the gas of your own transactions, and what the workspace will pay. Each step that uses your wallet first asks it to switch to Arc mainnet, and to add Arc mainnet if the wallet does not know it yet.

1. **Prove the wallet is yours.** Choose **Connect your wallet**. If your browser has several wallets, choose one first. Your wallet asks you to connect, then to sign a message that names the workspace, Arc mainnet and your wallet's address. Signing sends nothing. The page confirms: "Your wallet is this workspace's treasury." Vestiarion creates the agent's wallet at the same time.
2. **Step 2 of 3, "Set up your wallet as the treasury".** It shows what is set up so far, read from the chain, then the next of four steps:
   - **The agent's wallet.** Vestiarion created it with your choice: the only wallet that can pay through your contract, in its own Circle account. It holds only gas, never your USDC. If Circle could not create it then, the page says "The agent's wallet was not created yet; create it below." Choose **Create the agent's wallet**.
   - **Deploy your contract.** Enter a **Daily figure (USDC)** and a **7-day figure (USDC)**, or leave either empty to use that figure of the agent's spending limit. Then choose **Deploy from your wallet** and confirm in your wallet. Once the chain shows the contract, Vestiarion checks that it is its spending limit contract for your wallet and the agent, then records it.
   - **Approve your contract.** Choose **Approve from your wallet** and confirm. This lets the contract move your wallet's USDC, and it still pays nothing past its figures. Leave **Cap (USDC)** empty to approve without a cap, or enter one.
   - **Give the agent its gas.** On Arc mainnet the agent pays its own gas in USDC. Choose **Send 0.50 USDC for gas** and confirm. Each payment costs it well under 0.01 USDC, and going live needs it to hold at least 0.10 USDC.
3. **Step 3 of 3, "Check your wallet, then go live".** It shows your wallet and the USDC in it, the agent's wallet and its gas, your contract and its figures, and what the agent can move: your wallet's USDC, or the approval if that is less. Type `mainnet` in **Type mainnet to confirm**, then confirm **Go live**, as in [step 4](#4-go-live).

#### With a passkey

You need USDC on Arc mainnet to send to the new wallet: about 0.75 USDC for its setup at first, and what the workspace will pay once its recovery phrase is saved.

1. **Create the wallet.** Choose **Create with a passkey**, and create the passkey when your browser asks. The page confirms: "Your passkey wallet is this workspace's treasury." Vestiarion creates the agent's wallet at the same time. If you made a passkey wallet for this workspace before, choose **Use a passkey you made before** instead.
2. **Step 2 of 3, "Set up your passkey wallet as the treasury".** It shows what is set up so far, read from the chain, then the next of three steps:
   - **Add USDC to your wallet.** Send USDC on Arc mainnet to the address under **Your wallet**, from an exchange or another wallet, and not to the agent's wallet: setup pays the agent's gas from yours. Setup needs about 0.75 USDC: 0.50 for the agent's gas, and up to 0.25 set aside for the network fee, of which about 0.07 is spent. The rest stays with the wallet for its next confirmations. Add only this for now; send what the workspace will pay once the recovery phrase is saved. The page checks for it every 10 seconds.
   - **Set up with one confirmation.** The **Daily figure (USDC)** and **7-day figure (USDC)** start from the agent's spending limit; change them if you like, and enter a **Cap (USDC)** if you want one. Choose **Set up with your passkey** and confirm once. In one transaction your wallet deploys its contract, sets these figures in it, approves it, and sends the agent 0.50 USDC for gas, paying the network fee from its own USDC. If the agent's wallet holds its gas already, that part is left out. A passkey prompt shows no transaction, so before it is asked, the page builds the transaction itself from the figures it shows and checks that it matches what Vestiarion sent. Anything else is refused: "The setup Vestiarion sent is not the one this page expected; nothing was signed."
   - **Save a recovery phrase.** Choose **Create a recovery phrase**. The page shows twelve words, made in your browser and never sent to Vestiarion. They can sign for the wallet on their own: anyone who has them can move every USDC in it, so write them on paper and keep them offline. Vestiarion will never ask for them. Tick "I wrote these twelve words down and keep them offline.", and choose **Register with your passkey**. If this passkey is ever lost, the words let you add a new one and keep the wallet. You may instead choose **Skip: I understand that losing this passkey loses this wallet**. Going live needs one or the other.
   - **Only when needed.** If Circle could not create the agent's wallet with your choice, step 2 starts with **Create the agent's wallet**. If the agent holds less than 0.10 USDC of gas before you go live, step 2 shows **Give the agent its gas**: choose **Send 0.50 USDC for gas** and confirm with your passkey.
3. **Step 3 of 3, "Check your wallet, then go live",** as with a wallet in your browser.

If you reload or close the page while the setup is confirming, the page says "Your setup was sent" and checks the same setup when you come back, before anything else. If it was never recorded and you set it up again, perhaps from another browser, the same contract is used and the agent's gas is not sent twice: only one more network fee is spent. Your device or password manager keeps the passkey, and this browser keeps only its public part, so the next step signs without asking you to pick it first. In another browser the page asks you to choose the passkey, and refuses one that owns another wallet: "Use the passkey you made for it."

On either route, once the workspace is live, these points differ from [What changes when you go live](#what-changes-when-you-go-live):

- **Every payment goes through your contract.** That includes the agent's own and the ones people approve with **Approve and pay** or **Pay now**. A person's payment the contract would refuse is refused before anything is sent, with the reason, for example "Paying 30 USDC would pass the contract's daily limit of 50 USDC: 40 USDC paid so far."
- **Only USDC on Arc mainnet is paid from it.** A payment in EURC, or to another chain, is refused: "Only USDC on Arc mainnet can be paid from your wallet's contract."
- **The figures are your contract's, and your wallet changes them.** In **Go live**, under **Change what the agent may pay**, enter the **Daily figure (USDC)** and **7-day figure (USDC)**, then choose **Change with your passkey**, or **Change from your wallet** with a wallet in your browser. Your wallet calls the contract's `setLimits` and pays its network fee. Once Arc mainnet shows the new figures, the agent's spending limit follows them, and a payment held under a lower figure is decided again. The console's agent spending limit shows them, with a link here.
- **The console's cash is what the contract can move**: your wallet's USDC, or the approval if that is less.
- **To stop payments from your wallet**, choose **Stop the agent's payments** in **Go live** and confirm. Your wallet sets its USDC approval for the contract to 0, so the contract can move nothing, and the agent is paused. **Resume payments** approves the contract again, without a cap or up to the **Cap (USDC)** you enter, and lifts that pause. Pausing the agent from the console works as in any workspace.

- **Money coming in is not read yet.** A client's payment into your wallet reaches you, but Vestiarion does not match it to its receivable yet, so the receivable stays open. Leave payment reminders off for this workspace's receivables until it does.

## What changes when you go live

- **The agent runs on a schedule.** A live workspace's agent runs a cycle every 6 hours on its own, with no one pressing a button. It also runs one, usually within a minute, after anything that gives it a decision to make, such as a payable added or returned to it. An owner or admin can still run one at any time with **Run cycle now** on the console. The console is **Treasury** in the app's navigation.
- **Payments move testnet USDC on Arc** from the operating wallet, within the guardrails. Anything the agent will not pay waits in **Approvals**.
- **The workspace is kept**, however long it is inactive.
- **Going live is one-way.** Nothing in the app switches a live workspace back to sandbox.

**To stop the agent**, go to the console and choose **Pause agent**. You can give a reason, which is shown on every page and kept in the ledger. A paused agent runs no cycle and moves no money, and its scheduled runs are skipped. People can still pay or reject from **Approvals**. Owners, admins and approvers can pause the agent. Only owners and admins can start it again, with **Resume agent**.

## On Arc mainnet

A deployment can open Arc mainnet to the people on its allowlist, or to everyone. Every other workspace stays on Arc testnet, and none moves between the two networks.

- **A workspace on Arc mainnet is chosen when it is created.** On the workspaces page, a person Arc mainnet is open to chooses **Arc mainnet** under **Network**. The workspace starts with one empty operating account, and an agent spending limit of 50 USDC a day and 150 USDC in 7 days. On Arc mainnet a daily or 7-day figure always stays.
- **Two people above 100 USDC.** A workspace on Arc mainnet starts with two approvals above 100 USDC, under **Settings > Security**. An owner can raise the figure but not turn it off there. With only one person who can approve payments, nothing above it is paid until the figure is raised or another approver is added.
- **It never simulates.** It has no sample data and no simulated balance, and nothing moves until an owner takes it live.
- **Step 1 offers your own wallet, or takes a live key.** Where the deployment offers it, your own wallet comes first, as [path C](#path-c-your-own-wallet) describes. With your own Circle account, paste a live API key, which starts with `LIVE_API_KEY`, and the entity secret registered for it. They come from your Circle account once it is upgraded to production. A test key is refused there, as a live key is refused on Arc testnet. Hosted wallets do not run on Arc mainnet.
- **With your own Circle account, step 2 creates one wallet.** It is an EOA on Arc mainnet, which pays its own gas in USDC, so the agent keeps 0.10 USDC of it aside for gas. A milestone release the rest cannot cover is held, and decided again once cash comes in.
- **A stuck payment holds back the ones after it.** An EOA sends its payments in order, and one whose network fee was too low waits until it is resolved. Vestiarion tells you of a payment that has not confirmed 15 minutes after it was sent, as [Your first payment](/docs/guides/first-payment) describes. When the console says "Circle shows it stuck.", look the transaction up in Circle's console by its hash, or contact Circle support. On path C the agent's wallet is in Vestiarion's own Circle account, so look the transaction up on Arc mainnet's explorer instead. Once it confirms or is cancelled, the later payments go out.
- **Step 3 has no faucet.** With your own Circle account, send USDC on Arc mainnet to the operating wallet's address. On path C your wallet holds it already. To go live, type `mainnet` in **Type mainnet to confirm**, then confirm **Go live**. From then on the agent's payments, and the ones people approve, move real USDC.
- **Every page and message names Arc mainnet.** Emails to payees and clients, the pay link, the payee's page, receipts, Slack, Telegram and pull request comments name the workspace's network. The USYC reserve, escrow and Gateway, which Arc mainnet does not have, are not shown.
- **Each step needs a person Arc mainnet is open to.** Anyone else is told "Arc mainnet is not open to this account yet."
- **Some features are not on Arc mainnet yet.** Payouts to other chains, the USYC reserve, the EURC swap, escrow, enforcing the spending limit in a contract from your own Circle account, and paying in one batch each say so by name. On path C your contract carries every payment. Approving a payment from Slack or Telegram is refused there: approve it in Vestiarion.
- **When the deployment switches Arc mainnet off**, every workspace on it holds: nothing moves, and the Go live section says "Arc mainnet is switched off on this deployment."

## Earn on idle cash with USYC

Every cycle, the agent decides whether idle USDC should earn in a reserve or come back to the operating wallet before payments fall due. Until you turn the **USYC reserve** on, that reserve is simulated. Turned on, the agent buys real USYC, Circle's tokenized money market fund, on Arc testnet.

1. **Get the wallets allowlisted.** USYC is permissioned: Circle allowlists the wallets that may hold it. **Settings** → **USYC reserve** shows the two addresses: the operating wallet, which buys USYC, and the reserve wallet, which holds and sells it. Ask Circle Support to allowlist both for USYC on Arc testnet.
2. **Turn it on.** An owner or admin chooses **Turn on**. Vestiarion first checks on Arc testnet that both wallets are allowlisted, and names any that is not. It also refuses while the simulated reserve still holds anything, so no simulated balance is ever counted as USYC. The change is signed in the audit log as `usyc_reserve_enabled`. It cannot be turned off from the app.
3. **Let the agent decide.** On its next cycles:
   - **A sweep.** The operating wallet approves USYC's Teller contract for the USDC, then deposits it, and the USYC goes to the reserve wallet.
   - **A redemption.** The reserve wallet sells the USYC it needs, and the USDC comes back to the operating wallet.

   Each move is a real transaction, linked from its decision card on the console.

**Payments come first.** Each cycle, before it decides any payment, the agent adds up the USDC payables due today and the verified milestones waiting to be paid, except one whose USDC is locked in escrow. When the operating wallet holds less, it sells the USYC the difference needs first, so those payments are made in that cycle rather than held. The audit log records the move as `cash_brought_back`. A payment still held because the cash was not there is decided again at the first cycle after cash comes in: from the faucet, from a redemption, or from **Bring cash back**. The model that decides sweeps and redemptions is told the same: every payment leaves from the operating wallet, and the reserve's cash counts toward a payment only once it is back there.

**To bring cash back yourself**, an owner or admin opens **Settings** → **USYC reserve**, enters an amount in **Amount (USDC)**, or leaves it empty for everything, and chooses **Bring cash back**. The reserve wallet sells that much USYC at its latest price, at any hour, and the USDC comes back to the operating wallet. The audit log records it as `cash_brought_back`, by you. Vestiarion then runs a cycle, so payments waiting for cash are made within a minute, unless the agent is paused.

The agent then sweeps nothing back into USYC for 24 hours, so the cash stays in the operating wallet for what you brought it back for. The written policy's reasoning says so, for example "A person brought 152.211756 USDC back from the reserve at 2026-10-05 09:05 UTC, so the agent sweeps nothing until 2026-10-06 09:05 UTC." A sweep the model chooses anyway is not made, and its decision says why. Redemptions for what falls due go on as before.

**A person's payment draws on the reserve too.** When **Approve and pay**, or **Pay now** on a held milestone, needs more than the operating wallet holds, it brings the difference back from the reserve first, then pays. You do not need to bring cash back yourself for it.

What to expect:

- **When USYC can be bought.** Only between its daily price update, around 08:30 New York time, and 14:00 New York time, on business days. Outside that window the agent is told it cannot sweep. A sweep it decides anyway is not made, and the decision says why. Redemptions are possible at any time.
- **What the reserve is worth.** Every cycle reads it from the chain: the reserve wallet's USYC at the latest price. The reserve no longer shows "simulated", and its yield is the fund's own over the last days, which is what the agent weighs a sweep against.
- **When a sweep pays.** The agent sweeps only when the yield beats what the two transfers cost. It counts the yield over how long the swept cash would stay, up to 30 days: what falls due is paid from the operating wallet first, and only what it cannot cover comes back from the reserve, on its day.
- **Bounded moves.** Code sets how much the agent may move. A sweep never takes the operating wallet below its buffer for what falls due within 7 days. A redemption brings back at most what falls due within 14 days needs, with a 15% cushion, and at least what the written policy redeems. The model is told these bounds. A move outside them is brought within them, and its audit log entry keeps what the model chose, as `boundedByCode`.
- **The contracts.** USYC, its Teller and the allowlist's Entitlements contract, with their addresses, are on [Contracts on Arc testnet](/docs/contracts).
- **Fees.** Buying and selling cost nothing for these wallets on Arc testnet, and Circle's Gas Station pays the network fee. The price rises day by day as the fund earns, about 3.4% a year at the end of September 2026.

## When something fails

A failed step shows one of these messages under its button. You can repeat any step. Wallets created before a failure are kept, and creating the wallets again adds only the missing ones.

| Message | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Paste both the API key and the entity secret. | Both fields are required. Paste each value in full. |
| Circle did not accept this API key. | Check that you copied the whole key and that it is still active in the Circle console. It must be for the workspace's network: a test key on Arc testnet, a live key on Arc mainnet. |
| This Circle API key is for Arc mainnet (LIVE_API_KEY). This workspace is on Arc testnet: paste a test key (TEST_API_KEY). | A workspace on Arc testnet takes a test key. In the Circle console, copy an API key from the testnet side: it starts with `TEST_API_KEY`. |
| Could not reach Circle; try again. | Circle did not answer in time. Try the step again in a moment. |
| This workspace's wallets belong to the connected Circle account; use its credentials. | The credentials belong to a different Circle account from the one that holds the wallets. Use that account's API key and entity secret. |
| Connect Circle first. | Step 1 is not done. Choose the hosted wallet, or connect your Circle account. |
| Circle did not accept the entity secret; reconnect with the right one. | The entity secret is not the one registered to the API key's account. Open **Replace Circle credentials**, paste the registered secret, then choose **Create treasury wallets** again. |
| Create the treasury wallets first. | Complete step 2, then go live. |
| This workspace is already live. | It went live already, from another tab or by another owner. Reload the page. |
| The stored Circle credentials cannot be read; reconnect. | Paste the API key and entity secret again under **Replace Circle credentials**. |
| The Circle credentials changed while going live; try again. | Someone replaced the credentials while this step was running. Reload the page and repeat the step. |
| This workspace has no operating wallet; it cannot take new credentials. | This appears when replacing the credentials of a live workspace. The new ones cannot be proven without an operating wallet, so the stored credentials stay. |
| Hosted testnet wallets are not available on this deployment. | Connect your own Circle account (path B). A workspace that already uses a hosted wallet pays nothing until hosted wallets are available again. |
| A workspace with its own Circle account or wallets cannot switch to a hosted wallet. | This workspace already has Circle credentials or wallets. Continue with your own account. |
| All hosted testnet wallets are taken; connect your own Circle account instead. | Follow path B with your own Circle account. |
| A hosted wallet does not run on this workspace's network yet. | Hosted wallets run on Arc testnet only; a workspace on another network connects its own Circle account. |
| This Circle API key is for Arc testnet (TEST_API_KEY). This workspace is on Arc mainnet: paste a live key (LIVE_API_KEY). | A workspace on Arc mainnet takes a live key from your Circle account's production side: it starts with `LIVE_API_KEY`. |

| No wallet was found in this browser. Install a wallet such as MetaMask or Rabby, then reload the page. | Path C needs a wallet in your browser. Install one, add your account to it, then reload. |
